# Reference content from planning conversations

Content developed while planning the badge, for use in modules and games. **Status key:** ✔ = checked against web sources (verification pass November 4, 2026; sources listed at the end). **verify** = not yet checked; confirm before shipping. **corrected** = the earlier version was wrong and has been fixed.

## Branch founding order ✔

1. Army: June 14, 1775
2. Navy: October 13, 1775
3. Marine Corps: November 10, 1775
4. Coast Guard: August 4, 1790, as the Revenue Cutter Service; renamed Coast Guard in 1915
5. Air Force: September 18, 1947, split from the Army
6. Space Force: December 20, 2019, split from the Air Force

Wrinkle ✔: the Continental Navy and Marines were disbanded after the Revolution. Congress re-established the Navy with the Naval Act of March 27, 1794, and President John Adams signed the act re-establishing the Marine Corps on July 11, 1798. Both officially date their birthdays to 1775. Fun facts: the Marines celebrated July 11 as their birthday until 1921, and Navy Day was October 27 (Theodore Roosevelt's birthday) until 1972.

## Role, purview and limits (verify)

| Branch | Department | Role | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Army | Army | Main land force; takes and holds ground over long campaigns; largest branch; has its own helicopters | Relies on Navy and Air Force to deploy overseas; covered by Posse Comitatus ✔ |
| Marine Corps | Navy | Expeditionary sea-to-land force; quick crisis response; guards U.S. embassies; own jets, helicopters, artillery | Built for short, intense operations; relies on the Navy for ships, doctors, corpsmen, chaplains |
| Navy | Navy | Sea control; keeps shipping lanes open; carriers project air power; submarines carry part of the nuclear deterrent | Operates at sea and along coasts; covered by Posse Comitatus since 2021 ✔ |
| Air Force | Air Force | Air superiority, long-range bombing, airlift; most land-based nuclear missiles and bombers | Can't hold ground; covered by Posse Comitatus ✔ |
| Space Force | Air Force | Operates and protects military satellites: GPS, missile warning, secure communications; tracks objects in orbit | Smallest branch; Guardians operate satellites from the ground; covered by Posse Comitatus ✔ |
| Coast Guard | Homeland Security in peacetime | Maritime law enforcement, search and rescue, port security, buoys, icebreakers | Small; limited combat role; can move under the Navy in wartime; not covered by Posse Comitatus ✔ |

National Guard: Army and Air National Guard answer to their governor unless federalized; under a governor, Posse Comitatus doesn't apply ✔.

Discussion question: why does the one branch that can arrest people sit outside the Department of Defense?

## "Which branch?" scenarios (verify)

**Clear-cut**
1. A fishing boat is sinking in a storm 50 miles off Alaska. → Coast Guard
2. Enemy boats and mines threaten oil tankers in a narrow strait. → Navy
3. The U.S. must hold and secure a country for years after a war. → Army
4. An enemy launches a ballistic missile; who spots it first? → Space Force (missile-warning satellites)
5. An earthquake overseas needs 500 tons of supplies within 24 hours. → Air Force airlift; Navy brings bigger loads later
6. Seize a beach on an island from the sea. → Marines
7. An enemy submarine shadows a U.S. carrier. → Navy
8. GPS is being jammed in a war zone. → Space Force

**Trickier**
9. A mob surrounds a U.S. embassy; staff need protecting and evacuating. → Marines
10. A Missouri River flood needs sandbaggers and trucks. → Missouri National Guard under the governor; not a federal branch
11. Smugglers in a speedboat in the Caribbean; a Navy ship spots them first. → Coast Guard; the Navy can carry a Coast Guard boarding team but can't make the arrest
12. Break a channel through Antarctic ice to resupply a research station. → Coast Guard icebreakers
13. Strike a target 6,000 miles away within hours. → Mostly Air Force bombers; Navy submarine missiles possible (debate faster vs. quieter)

**Debate rounds**
14. A pilot is shot down behind enemy lines; who rescues him? → Any branch with rescue aircraft; Air Force pararescue specializes
15. Why does the Army fly helicopters if the Air Force exists? → Direct ground support vs. the larger air war; overlap is deliberate
16. Plan D-Day: which branches and for what? → All; joint operations. The Air Force didn't exist yet (Army Air Forces in 1944)

## Rank differences (✔ except where marked)

- Navy and Coast Guard use naval ranks: Seaman → Petty Officer (sergeant-level) → Chief; officers Ensign, Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, Admiral.
- Captain gotcha: Navy Captain = O-6 (Army Colonel); Army Captain = O-3.
- **corrected** Staff Sergeant gotcha: Staff Sergeant is E-5 in the Air Force but E-6 in the Army and Marines. The Space Force has no Staff Sergeant; its E-5 is simply "Sergeant."
- Marines: Lance Corporal (E-3), Gunnery Sergeant ("Gunny," E-7).
- Air Force enlisted: Airman Basic, Airman, Airman First Class, Senior Airman.
- Space Force enlisted ✔: Specialist 1–4 (E-1 to E-4), Sergeant (E-5), then Technical, Master, Senior Master, and Chief Master Sergeant; adopted February 1, 2021. Officers use Air Force ranks.
- Army Specialist (E-4) is the technical-job alternative to Corporal.
- Navy enlisted named by job: "Boatswain's Mate Second Class" = Petty Officer Second Class on deck.
- Top enlisted: Sergeant Major of the Army; Master Chief Petty Officer of the Navy; Sergeant Major of the Marine Corps; Chief Master Sergeant of the Air Force; Chief Master Sergeant of the Space Force; Master Chief Petty Officer of the Coast Guard.
- **corrected** Five-star ranks ✔: nine officers total. General of the Army: Marshall, MacArthur, Eisenhower, Arnold (December 1944) and Bradley (1950). Fleet Admiral: Leahy, King, Nimitz (December 1944) and Halsey (December 1945). Arnold became General of the Air Force in 1949, the only one ever and the only officer with five stars in two branches. No one has held the rank since Bradley died in 1981.

## Holidays, observances and anniversaries

**Federal holidays ✔**
- Memorial Day, last Monday in May: those who died in service. Decoration Day, 1868. Flags at half-staff from sunrise until noon ✔; a National Moment of Remembrance at 3 p.m. local time, set by law in December 2000 ✔.
- Veterans Day, November 11: all who served. Armistice Day, 11 a.m. 11/11/1918; legal holiday 1938; renamed 1954; moved to October 1971–1977; back to November 11 in 1978.

**Observances ✔**
- Armed Forces Day: third Saturday in May; those serving now ✔. Within National Military Appreciation Month (May).
- Military Spouse Appreciation Day: Friday before Mother's Day.
- Month of the Military Child: April.
- National Medal of Honor Day: March 25.
- National Vietnam War Veterans Day: March 29.
- National Korean War Veterans Armistice Day: July 27.
- National POW/MIA Recognition Day: third Friday in September ✔.
- Gold Star Mother's and Family's Day: last Sunday in September ✔.
- Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day: December 7, designated by Congress August 23, 1994; flags at half-staff all day ✔.
- Purple Heart Day: August 7; widely observed, less formally established.

**Branch birthdays ✔:** Army June 14 (also Flag Day); Coast Guard August 4; Air Force September 18; Navy October 13; Marine Corps November 10 (birthday ball and cake-cutting); National Guard December 13 (1636 militia); Space Force December 20.

**Anniversaries ✔**
- Flag Day, June 14: flag adopted 1777; Wilson proclaimed 1916; Congress made it official 1949; observance, not a federal holiday.
- Pearl Harbor, December 7, 1941: 2,403 Americans killed and 1,178 wounded; Roosevelt's "date which will live in infamy" speech the next day.
- D-Day, June 6, 1944: Normandy invasion; about 156,000 troops; beaches Utah and Omaha (U.S.), Gold and Sword (British), Juno (Canadian, with British support); Eisenhower supreme commander.
- VE Day, May 8, 1945: Germany's surrender; Truman (of Independence) announced it on his 61st birthday. National holiday in Britain and France, not the U.S.
- VJ Day: Japan announced surrender August 14–15, 1945; formal signing aboard USS *Missouri*, September 2, 1945.
