# Military Heritage Trail Badge — Two-Meeting Leader Guide

## Overview

Two 75-minute meetings cover all five required items plus optional 10, 11, 20, 22 and 23. Each boy still needs one "Military Experience" item (6 or 7) to finish; a veteran guest at Meeting 1 can cover 7 if each boy asks his own questions and writes up the answers (mentor's call).

**Group:** ~8 Navigators and Adventurers (ages 11–17) in two patrols of 4. Mix ages, and make an Adventurer each patrol's captain. Stations and competitions, not lecture; one reference copy per station.

**Timing:** Veterans Day is Wednesday, November 11, and the Marine Corps birthday is November 10. Running Meeting 1 before then makes Requirement 9 (attend a ceremony) a natural follow-on.

**Heads-up on the badge sheet:** it's dated 2018 and says "five branches." There are six now — Space Force was created in December 2019. Use that as Meeting 1's opener rather than a correction.

Group exposure isn't the same as sign-off. Each meeting ends with a quick teach-back so the mentor can see which boys can actually explain each item.

## Meeting 1 — Who Serves (75 min)

Covers Requirements 1, 2, 5, 10, 11, and optionally 7. Everything runs patrol vs. patrol for points; the losing patrol stacks chairs.

| Time | Block | Req |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Opener: Five or six? | 1 |
| 0:05 | Branch timeline race | 1 |
| 0:20 | Academy run-and-sort | 2 |
| 0:32 | Floor calendar + GI Bill story | 5, 11 |
| 0:47 | Tomb Guard walk | 10 |
| 0:57 | Veteran guest Q&A (optional) | 7 |
| 1:10 | Teach-back + prayer | — |

**One leader?** Run the blocks in order with the whole group. **Two leaders?** Run the academy sort and floor calendar as side-by-side stations and swap patrols at 0:40.

### Opener: Five or six? (5 min)

Each patrol gets 60 seconds to write every branch of the Armed Forces on a whiteboard. Then read the badge sheet's line about "five branches" and ask who's right.

- Reveal six: Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, Space Force, Coast Guard. The sheet was printed in 2018; Space Force arrived in December 2019.
- Ask: which is oldest? Which isn't in the Department of Defense? (Coast Guard — it's under Homeland Security in peacetime.)

### Branch timeline race (15 min) — Req 1

Each patrol gets a shuffled deck of 24 cards: 6 branch names, 6 founding dates, 6 domains (land, sea, air, space, coast, sea-to-land), and 6 "what we do" cards. They lay a rope on the floor as a timeline and build one row per branch in founding order. One point per correct card; answers are in the fact sheet below.

Discussion while you score:

1. The Army fights on land and the Navy at sea. Why do we also need Marines?
2. The Air Force split from the Army in 1947, and Space Force split from the Air Force in 2019. What pattern do you see? Will there be a seventh branch?
3. The Coast Guard arrests drug smugglers; the Army can't arrest anyone at home. Why the difference? (Plant this — it's Meeting 2.)
4. The GPS on your parents' phones is run by Space Force. What else would stop working without it?

### Academy run-and-sort (12 min) — Req 2

Post three wall signs, each with the academy's name, founding year, location and branch. Read a famous graduate's name; boys run to the wall they think is right. A patrol scores when the majority of its members picked correctly.

- **West Point:** Ulysses S. Grant, Robert E. Lee, Douglas MacArthur, Dwight Eisenhower, George Patton, Buzz Aldrin
- **Naval Academy:** Chester Nimitz, Jimmy Carter, Alan Shepard, Jim Lovell
- **Air Force Academy:** Lance Sijan, Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger, Gregg Popovich

Save Aldrin and Popovich for last — a moonwalker from the Army's academy and an NBA coach from the Air Force's are good arguments. Bonus point: name the other two federal service academies (Coast Guard Academy in New London, CT; Merchant Marine Academy in Kings Point, NY).

### Floor calendar + GI Bill story (15 min) — Reqs 5, 11

Tape a 12-month grid on the floor. Patrols place holiday cards on the right month, then match each to an origin card. Then the key question: what's the difference between Memorial Day and Veterans Day?

The answer to drill: Memorial Day honors those who died in service; Veterans Day thanks everyone who served, living or dead; Armed Forces Day honors those serving now. Only the first two are federal holidays.

Close the station with a two-minute GI Bill story: in 1944 Congress paid for millions of returning soldiers to go to college and buy homes. Ask: how might your grandparents' or great-grandparents' lives have been different without it?

### Tomb Guard walk (10 min) — Req 10

Tell Arlington's story in three minutes (fact sheet): Robert E. Lee's estate, the first burial in May 1864, the Unknown Soldier in 1921, and a guard every minute since July 2, 1937.

Then tape a 21-step line. Each boy walks the sentinel's pattern: 21 steps, face east for 21 seconds, face north for 21 seconds, turn, 21 steps back. A judge from the other patrol counts and watches for fidgeting. Ask afterward: why guard a grave when no one knows who's in it?

### Veteran guest Q&A (13 min, optional) — Req 7

Have each boy write two questions in advance, covering the badge's three: why he enlisted (or was drafted), what he expected, and how reality differed. Each boy records the answers in his own words for his mentor. If there's no guest, use this time to plan which Veterans Day event the group will attend.

### Teach-back + prayer (5 min)

Each boy draws one card (a branch, an academy, or a holiday) and explains it to the group in 30 seconds. Close by praying for those serving now and for families who lost someone.

## Meeting 2 — Draft, Law & Strategy (75 min)

Covers Requirements 3, 4, 20, 22 and 23. Capture the flag is the centerpiece, so protect its 30 minutes; if time runs short, cut the rank race first.

| Time | Block | Req |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Birthday draft lottery | 3 |
| 0:12 | "Who can respond?" scenarios | 4 |
| 0:24 | Rank ladder race | 20 |
| 0:36 | Strategy session + capture the flag | 22 |
| 1:06 | Armor of God + teach-back | 23 |

### Birthday draft lottery (12 min) — Req 3

Put every boy's birthday on a slip, plus 20–30 family birthdays you've collected, in plastic eggs in a bucket. Draw them one at a time and post the order. Then explain: on December 1, 1969, the Selective Service did exactly this on live TV with every day of the year, and low numbers were called first.

Write three eras on the board and fill in the criteria together (answers in the fact sheet):

| Era | Law | Who was eligible | Notable rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Civil War | Enrollment Act of 1863 | Men 20–45 | Could hire a substitute or pay $300 to avoid service |
| World War I | Selective Service Act of 1917 | Men 21–30, later widened to 18–45 | First draft run through local boards |
| World War II | Selective Training and Service Act of 1940 | Men 21–35 at first registration | First peacetime draft in U.S. history |
| Vietnam | 1969 lottery amendment | Men by birth date | Random lottery; draft ended in 1973 |

Discussion:

1. Was letting a man pay $300 to stay home fair? Who could afford it?
2. Is a lottery fairer than letting local boards choose? Why or why not?
3. Bring it home for the Adventurers: men 18–25 must be registered with Selective Service, and starting December 2026 registration becomes automatic. Registration is not a draft — Congress would have to act before anyone is called.

### "Who can respond?" scenarios (12 min) — Req 4

Start with the Old West: a sheriff's *posse* comes from *posse comitatus*, Latin for "power of the county." The 1878 Posse Comitatus Act says federal troops can't be used to enforce laws at home unless the Constitution or Congress allows it.

Read each scenario. Patrols hold up a card: **green** (military can do it), **red** (civilian police only), **yellow** (only under a special exception).

1. A tornado flattens a Missouri town. The governor sends the National Guard to clear roads and keep order. *Green — a Guard under the governor's command isn't covered.*
2. Army soldiers set up checkpoints on I-70 and write speeding tickets. *Red.*
3. The Coast Guard boards a boat smuggling drugs off Florida. *Green — the Coast Guard is a law enforcement agency and isn't covered.*
4. The Navy shares radar data to help police track a smuggling plane. *Green — Congress allowed this kind of indirect support in 1981.*
5. In 1957, the President sends the 101st Airborne to protect nine Black students entering Little Rock Central High. *Yellow — legal under the Insurrection Act exception.*
6. Marines from a nearby base arrest a shoplifter at the mall. *Red.*

Debrief: why would Americans want a law keeping soldiers out of police work? Connect it to the Declaration of Independence's complaint about standing armies kept "among us, in times of peace."

**Leader note:** this law has been in the news since 2025 over domestic troop deployments. Keep the discussion on the principle and the history, not current events or parties.

### Rank ladder race (12 min) — Req 20

Use the Army. Each patrol gets two sets of insignia cards, enlisted (E-1 to E-9) and officer (O-1 to O-10), and races to tape them on the wall in ascending order. Answers are in the fact sheet.

The gotcha everyone misses: gold ranks below silver — second lieutenant (gold) is below first lieutenant (silver), and major (gold oak leaf) is below lieutenant colonel (silver oak leaf).

Then the difference, in two lines:

- **Officers** hold a commission (through an academy, ROTC or officer school), usually have a college degree, and plan and command.
- **Non-commissioned officers** are enlisted soldiers who rose through the ranks; they train and lead troops day to day — "the backbone of the Army."

Ask: in our troop, who's more like an officer, and who's more like a sergeant?

### Strategy session + capture the flag (30 min) — Req 22

**Plan (8 min).** Each patrol writes a plan on one sheet: a one-sentence *commander's intent*, roles (attack, defense, scout, jail guard), one silent signal, and a fallback if the flag carrier is tagged.

**Play (2 rounds × 7 min).** Two-hand touch, jail on each side, flags visible.

**After-action review (between rounds and at the end).** Use the Army's four questions:

1. What was supposed to happen?
2. What actually happened?
3. Why was there a difference?
4. What will we do differently next time?

Patrols revise their plan between rounds, then compare: did the change work? That second-round revision is the part that satisfies "what improvements could be used next time."

**November is dark by about 5:00 p.m.** Play in a gym, or outdoors with glow sticks on flags and players, a lit boundary, and buddy pairs.

### Armor of God + teach-back (9 min) — Req 23

Read Ephesians 6:13–17 while a volunteer is "suited up" with paper labels or real gear: belt, body armor, boots, shield, helmet, sword. Match each to Paul's list: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the Word of God.

Ask which is most crucial, and make each boy defend his answer. Two points worth raising: the sword is the only offensive piece, and Roman shields locked together into a wall — faith works best in a group. Note that Paul ends with prayer (verse 18), like a soldier's radio back to headquarters.

Finish with the 30-second teach-back: each boy explains one of tonight's items to the group.

## Leader fact sheet

Answer key for every card deck and discussion question; facts checked against current sources (listed at the end).

### Branches (Req 1)

| Branch | Founded | Domain | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Army | June 14, 1775 | Land | Sustained ground combat; the largest branch |
| Navy | October 13, 1775 | Sea | Controls the seas; carriers, submarines, ships |
| Marine Corps | November 10, 1775 | Sea-to-land | Expeditionary crisis response; amphibious assault |
| Coast Guard | August 4, 1790 | Coast | Law enforcement at sea, search and rescue, port security; under Homeland Security in peacetime |
| Air Force | September 18, 1947 | Air | Air superiority, airlift, long-range strike; split from the Army |
| Space Force | December 20, 2019 | Space | Satellites, GPS, missile warning; split from the Air Force |

The Coast Guard began as the Revenue Cutter Service and took its current name in 1915.

### Academies (Req 2)

| Academy | Founded | Location | Commissions into | Graduates to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U.S. Military Academy (West Point) | 1802 | West Point, NY | Army | Grant, Lee, MacArthur, Eisenhower, Patton, Buzz Aldrin |
| U.S. Naval Academy | 1845 | Annapolis, MD | Navy, Marine Corps | Nimitz, Jimmy Carter, Alan Shepard, Jim Lovell |
| U.S. Air Force Academy | 1954 | Colorado Springs, CO | Air Force, Space Force | Lance Sijan (Medal of Honor), Sully Sullenberger, Gregg Popovich |

### Military holidays and observances (Req 5)

| Day | When | Honors | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Memorial Day (federal holiday) | Last Monday in May | Those who died in service | Decoration Day: Gen. John A. Logan's General Order No. 11 set May 30, 1868; moved to a Monday in 1971 |
| Veterans Day (federal holiday) | November 11 | All who served, living or dead | Armistice Day, marking WWI's end at 11 a.m. on 11/11/1918; legal holiday 1938; renamed by Eisenhower in 1954 |
| Armed Forces Day | Third Saturday in May | Those serving now | Replaced separate branch days after the services were unified; not a day off |
| National POW/MIA Recognition Day | Third Friday in September | Prisoners of war and the missing | Observance, not a holiday |
| Gold Star Mother's and Family's Day | Last Sunday in September | Families who lost a service member | Observance, not a holiday |
| Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day | December 7 | Those killed at Pearl Harbor, 1941 | Observance, not a holiday |

Good trivia: Veterans Day was moved to the fourth Monday in October starting in 1971. It was unpopular, and it returned to November 11 in 1978.

### Arlington National Cemetery (Req 10)

- The land was Arlington House, the estate of Robert E. Lee's wife's family, taken by the Union during the Civil War.
- First military burial: Pvt. William Henry Christman, 67th Pennsylvania Infantry, May 13, 1864. He died of measles, not in battle.
- The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier was dedicated November 11, 1921. It has been guarded every minute since July 2, 1937, and by the 3rd U.S. Infantry ("The Old Guard") since 1948.
- Current duty: more than 400,000 people are buried there, with about 6,900 burials a year.

### GI Bill (Req 11)

The Servicemen's Readjustment Act, signed by Franklin Roosevelt on June 22, 1944, paid for college or job training, guaranteed low-cost home loans, and gave unemployment pay to returning WWII veterans. It helped drive the postwar boom in college enrollment and suburban homeownership. Today's version is the Post-9/11 GI Bill.

### Conscription (Req 3)

- **Definition:** conscription (the draft) is the government requiring people to serve in the military.
- **1863:** the Enrollment Act covered men 20–45. A man could hire a substitute or pay $300; anger over that sparked the New York City draft riots that July.
- **1917:** the Selective Service Act registered men 21–30, later widened to 18–45. About 24 million registered and 2.8 million were drafted.
- **1940:** the first peacetime draft, signed September 16, 1940; the first registration covered men 21–35. About 10 million were drafted by the war's end.
- **1969–1973:** the first Vietnam lottery was drawn December 1, 1969; the draft ended in 1973.
- **Now:** men 18–25 must be registered. The FY2026 defense bill, signed December 18, 2025, makes registration automatic starting December 2026.

### Posse Comitatus Act (Req 4)

Signed by President Rutherford B. Hayes on June 18, 1878, after Reconstruction, the Act bars using the federal military to enforce domestic law unless the Constitution or Congress allows it. Violations carry a fine or up to two years in prison.

- Originally covered only the Army; the Air Force was added in 1956.
- The Navy and Marine Corps followed the rule by Defense Department policy until Congress wrote them, and Space Force, into the law in December 2021.
- Not covered: the Coast Guard (a law enforcement agency) and the National Guard under a governor's command.
- Main exception: the Insurrection Act, used in Little Rock in 1957.

## Beyond the meeting

The two meetings leave each boy needing one Military Experience item (6 or 7) unless the veteran guest counts. These are the easiest follow-ons from Independence.

| Req | Option | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | Interview a family veteran | Easiest path; use the same three badge questions from Meeting 1 |
| 6 | Recruiter visit with a parent | Best for older Adventurers who are actually curious |
| 9 | Veterans Day ceremony, Wed. Nov 11 | Check the National WWI Museum and local VFW/American Legion posts |
| 8 | Troop service project | Holiday cards or care packages for a local veterans' home |
| 15 | National WWI Museum and Memorial, Kansas City | Strong group outing; Truman Library in Independence is a WWI tie-in (Truman commanded an artillery battery) |
| 12, 17 | Wilson's Creek National Battlefield, near Springfield | About 2.5–3 hours. Union Gen. Nathaniel Lyon was killed there in 1861, which makes him a ready answer for 17 |
| 18 | Ulysses S. Grant's early life | Missouri tie: he farmed and sold firewood near St. Louis before the war; the Grant National Historic Site is there |
| 16 | Navy Hymn, "Eternal Father, Strong to Save" | Written in 1860; good 5-minute add-on to any meeting |
